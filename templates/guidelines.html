How to perform

A careful run is a meaningful result.

Follow this sequence for the physical apparatus or the virtual lab. The virtual controls deliberately mirror the experimental logic.

PrepareReach steady stateCooling readingsCalculationPrecautions

1. Prepare the arrangement

  1. Measure the diameter and thickness of the specimen using a vernier calliper and screw gauge. Convert all dimensions to metres.
  2. Measure the mass of the Lee’s disc. Use the supplied specific heat capacity for its material, typically brass.
  3. Place the dry specimen centrally between the steam chamber and Lee’s disc. Ensure the contact faces are clean and parallel.
  4. Place thermometers in the steam chamber and Lee’s disc sockets so their bulbs make proper thermal contact.

2. Reach steady state

  1. Start the boiler and allow steam to pass through the chamber.
  2. Observe both thermometer readings at regular intervals. The disc warms gradually.
  3. Wait until the Lee’s-disc temperature becomes nearly constant for several successive readings. Record T₁ (steam-side) and T₂ (disc).
In the virtual lab: choose sample dimensions, start the heater, then wait for the “steady state” indicator before beginning a cooling run.

3. Obtain a cooling curve

  1. Quickly remove the steam chamber and specimen from the Lee’s disc.
  2. Record the disc temperature against time as it cools through values close to T₂.
  3. Find the gradient |dT/dt| near T₂ using two readings on either side of T₂, or a tangent to the cooling graph.

The virtual lab stores a small set of cooling points and estimates this gradient by linear regression near the recorded steady temperature.

4. Calculate and report

  1. Calculate the specimen area A = πd²/4.
  2. Substitute m, s, x, A, T₁, T₂, and |dT/dt| in the working equation.
  3. Report k in W m⁻¹ K⁻¹ to appropriate significant figures. Compare it to an accepted range only as a reasonableness check.

Essential precautions

  • Do not touch the hot steam chamber or metal disc in a physical lab. Use tongs or heat-resistant handling methods.
  • Prevent water droplets from collecting on the contact surfaces; they change the heat-transfer path.
  • Keep the specimen exactly centred and use no excessive pressure that could compress it.
  • Begin cooling readings immediately after removing the steam chamber.
  • Read thermometers at eye level and use consistent time intervals.
  • Use the cooling rate at the same temperature as the steady Lee’s-disc temperature — not at an arbitrary point on the curve.
